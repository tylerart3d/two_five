# The Road to Vietnam — next chapter outline

Status: research-backed editorial draft, 2026-09-07. Not yet connected to the
live chapter selector, event loader, map, or PDF viewer.

## Chapter boundary

Working title: **The Road to Vietnam**.

Main coverage: **1 January–13 April 1966**, with a short bridge back to the
December 1965 deployment preparations. The previous chapter retains its
July–December coverage; the bridge does not duplicate those events as new ones.

Narrative arc: an understrength battalion assembles for deployment, crosses the
Pacific, trains on Okinawa, and arrives in Vietnam. End at Chu Lai on April 13.
The April 14 assumption of a tactical area of responsibility around Hill 69
provides the opening of the following chapter.

Use **Okinawa**, specifically Camp Schwab and the Northern Training Area, in
the historical text and map. Brent recalled training in Japan; these sources
identify Okinawa. No mainland-Japan training stop is established by the three
chronologies reviewed. Do not add one without evidence.

## Narrative blocks

### 01 / Preparing to leave

The battalion's departure required a rapid build-up. On December 17 it had been
designated a battalion landing team for planning, and the landing team was
activated on January 1. The commander's report describes the battalion as below
half its deployable strength in mid-December; 534 enlisted Marines joined between
December 20 and January 8. Training competed with the work of obtaining equipment
and preparing to embark. This connects the rebuilding at Pendleton to the
deployment that followed. [ROAD-01, ROAD-02; 1201048066, OCR pp. 3–4]

### 02 / Across the Pacific

Cargo loading began at Long Beach on January 7. Troops embarked on January 9,
and ships sailed from Pier E on January 10, bound for Okinawa by way of Pearl
Harbor. The chronology records arrival at Pearl Harbor on January 18 and
departure two days later. Shipboard training consisted of formal instruction
and physical training, including Vietnam orientation and landmine warfare.
The battalion arrived on Okinawa on February 2; the same record dates the
arrival of USS Mathews and USS Belle Grove to February 4. The movement should
therefore be shown as several ship movements, not a single simultaneous arrival.
[ROAD-03, ROAD-04; 1201048066, OCR pp. 5, 8]

### 03 / Training on Okinawa

Camp Schwab became the battalion's base for an extensive training period.
Each company spent eight days at the Counter-Guerrilla Warfare School in the
Northern Training Area, with formal instruction, patrolling, and a two-day
guerrilla problem. The program also included day and night live firing,
landmine and demolition instruction, jungle lanes, helicopter training, and
amphibious-vehicle training. The commander reported that personnel had stabilized
and unit cohesion had improved. These are the commander's assessments, rather
than an independent judgment that every Marine was equally prepared.
[ROAD-05 through ROAD-08; 1201048067, OCR pp. 2–6]

### 04 / Bound for Chu Lai

BLT 2/5 was activated again on March 25, and operational and administrative
control shifted to the 9th Marine Amphibious Brigade on March 30. Before departure,
the landing team conducted a four-day amphibious landing exercise at Kin Beach.
It left Okinawa on April 8 and arrived at Chu Lai on April 13. After debarkation,
the landing team was dissolved and its support units returned to their parent
commands. Arrival closes this chapter; the battalion's first assigned area and
operations belong in the next one. [ROAD-09, ROAD-10; 1201048067, OCR p. 6;
1201048068, OCR p. 2]

2. Long Beach: embarkation and sailing; source names Pier E.
3. Pearl Harbor: January 18–20 stop.
4. Camp Schwab and Northern Training Area, Okinawa: primary training scene.
5. Kin Beach: amphibious training before sailing.
6. Chu Lai: arrival; reserve Hill 69 operational detail for the next chapter.

Coordinates and period boundaries for the new locations remain unverified.
